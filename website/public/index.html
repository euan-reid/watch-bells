Watch Bells

Traditional ship's bells for your computer.

Watch Bells rings ship's bells every half hour on your Mac, Windows, or Linux computer. It sits in your menu bar or system tray, where you can mute it whenever you need some quiet.

The number of bells tells you how far through the watch you are.

Download Watch Bells View source code

Using Watch Bells

Open the app and look for the bell icon in your menu bar or system tray. Hover over it to see the current watch and bell count, or open its menu to mute the bells or quit.

Sound Mute when you need to

Select Mute in the bell menu. Select it again to turn the sound back on.

Startup Start with your computer

Add Watch Bells to your startup apps. See the installation notes.

Connection Works offline

The bell recordings are included in the download.

How ship's bells tell time

One bell marks the first half-hour of a four-hour watch, two bells the first hour, and so on up to eight. The bells sound in pairs to make them easier to count, with a single strike at the end for odd numbers.

The seven watches

Five watches last four hours each. Two shorter dog watches split the early evening. That gives an odd number of watches, so crews taking turns need not work the same hours every day.

The dog watches

The two dog watches interrupt the ordinary four-hour progression. In the Last Dog Watch, Watch Bells also follows the British convention associated with the Nore mutiny of 1797: five bells at 18:30 are omitted, the reputed signal for the mutiny to begin.

You can see the sequence below. At 18:30 the count returns to one; at 20:00, eight bells still marks the change to the First Watch.
